# ConnectAbroad: business and first-cohort operating plan

Planning date: 16 October 2026, Asia/Kolkata. Desired review date: 15 November 2026; live-user targets depend on a safe beta being ready.
The user has chosen a from-scratch build and a hard ₹0 additional-cash budget. No application backend, deployment, audience, or revenue is assumed to exist.
This extends the supplied product blueprint; it does not certify a working application, market validation, or market leadership.
All conversion rates, prices, costs, and decision thresholds below are hypotheses for this beta. They are not forecasts or financial guidance.

## Budget boundary: ₹0 additional cash

Use already available hardware/connectivity and free open-source software; keep the first prototype local or run supervised demos during staffed hours.
Do not buy hosting, a domain, ads, premium assets, APIs, email/SMS delivery, subscriptions, or trials requiring a payment card. No new paid agent/model access is required; use only access already available without incremental charges.
Optional free hosting is conditional on checking current quotas, account/card requirements, privacy, service limits, and avoiding billable overages; it is not a release dependency or an always-on guarantee.
If no suitable free public deployment is available, continue interviews, consented manual introductions, and local demonstrations instead of promising an always-available website.
Do not expose the founder's computer or private member data publicly to meet the date. Public deployment still requires the engineering/security release gates.
Track volunteer time as opportunity cost, separately from cash expenditure. No helper payout, refund liability, processor charge, or operational invoice is authorized by this budget.

## 1. The business decision

Build a dependable introduction and reply loop for one reachable community before building a broad social network.
The first offer is: “Find someone from your community who has lived the move you are planning, ask a specific question, and understand their perspective.”
The initial job is practical lived-experience sharing: choosing questions to ask a university, understanding everyday settling-in experiences, and finding relevant peers.
Do not sell admissions outcomes, visa decisions, jobs, accommodation availability, or professional advice.
The defensible asset, if it develops, is a responsive community with clear affiliations and repeatable useful introductions; a beautiful directory alone is easy to copy.

## 2. Choose one corridor from actual access

Provisional candidate: students and alumni of the founder's own reachable Indian college, planning a master's or their first semester in one destination city already represented by alumni.
India is a working launch assumption, not a fact established by the source document. Do not choose a country solely because it appears popular online.
No named college or city can honestly be declared validated from the files available here.
By 18 October, the founder must replace this candidate with one named origin institution/community, one destination city, and one near-term journey.

Evaluate at most three candidate corridors using the following short evidence sheet:

| Evidence needed | Minimum to choose the corridor |
| --- | --- |
| Reachable supply | Five residents/current students personally confirm interest; a path to eight by the first release |
| Reachable demand | Ten prospective movers describe a real current question; short confirmations may supplement the eight full seeker interviews |
| Distribution access | One alumni/community administrator agrees to review a small beta invitation |
| Shared relevance | Five seekers independently describe an overlapping question and accept a relevant introduction |
| Sustainable help | Helpers agree on topics, capacity, privacy, response expectations, and opt-out |

Record who confirmed, when, and what they consented to; keep the contact list private.
Select the corridor with the most confirmed access, not the largest theoretical market.
If no corridor passes, keep recruitment in interview mode and continue a consented manual pilot; do not populate a fake worldwide network.
Publish the exact cohort in landing-page copy only after resident availability and working discovery are confirmed.

## 3. Position against actual alternatives

These are current official product descriptions checked during planning, not independent quality rankings.

| Alternative | Existing offer | What ConnectAbroad must test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unibuddy | Institution-oriented student ambassador profiles, peer conversations, and community engagement. [Official platform](https://unibuddy.com/) | Whether a small cross-institution/shared-origin cohort produces relevant, candid conversations; institutional affiliation must be explicit |
| MentR-Me | Alumni/current-student matching with 30/60-minute calls for school selection and application-related support. [Official mentor page](https://mentr-me.com/find-mentor) | Whether a lighter community introduction is useful before someone needs a structured paid session |
| Yocket | Country/course groups, students/alumni connections, study-abroad tools, and premium expert support. [Official app page](https://consultation.yocket.com/app) | Whether the chosen cohort gets a relevant reply with less search effort; do not duplicate the entire study-abroad suite |
| Existing alumni groups and personal networks | The user's current workaround, to document in interviews | Why someone would return here after an introduction instead of moving every interaction elsewhere |

Do not claim “first,” “only,” “unbiased,” “verified,” or “best” without evidence supporting the precise claim.
Ask interviewees to complete the same small task using their current approach and the beta: find a relevant willing person and ask one question.
Compare time, reply quality, privacy comfort, and whether they would choose the beta for the next question.

## 4. What Reddit contributes, and what it cannot prove

Two discussions were read, including comments and counterexamples:

- In a discussion about Unibuddy, participants worry about university incentives; other self-described ambassadors report honest answers, and one describes volunteering. The research implication is to disclose affiliations and offer more than one perspective. It does not establish that a company or its ambassadors are dishonest. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/UniUK/comments/1r81iwl/are_students_on_unibuddy_telling_the_truth/)
- A study-abroad consultancy discussion contains complaints about generic support and commercial incentives, but also favorable accounts and disagreement about the value of paid help. Test whether users value a specific peer introduction, rather than assuming that criticism of consultants equals demand for this product. [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Indians_StudyAbroad/comments/1m9mooc/i_worked_at_a_popular_study_abroad_consultancy/)

These are self-selected, unverified anecdotes. Upvotes, complaints, and a few supportive comments do not measure market size, conversion, willingness to pay, or prevalence of scams.
Do not copy Reddit identities into outreach lists, contact commenters without an appropriate invitation, or repeat accusations as verified facts.
Use the threads to improve interview questions; use the founder's own consenting cohort to validate demand.

## 5. Supply first: make an introduction worth accepting

Five helper commitments are enough to choose a corridor; eight relevant helpers with confirmed current availability are required before opening the first beta cohort.
“Helper” describes a beta role, not a professional credential. A commitment alone does not count as a registration or current capacity.
Offer a clearly bounded contribution: three asynchronous new question threads per week, roughly 15 minutes each, with a 72-hour target for a first substantive answer.
Ask each helper to choose their own cap; three is only the initial planning assumption. Zero capacity means pause introductions.
Three threads means three people helped, not an unlimited number of follow-up messages.
Never promise a response time to seekers unless helpers accept it and the pilot demonstrates it.

The founder keeps a private availability sheet: helper ID, topics, destination, language if volunteered, affiliation, weekly cap, introductions used, availability, and consent date.
Verify claimed community relevance with a consensual mutual introduction or narrowly scoped evidence; do not retain passports, immigration documents, or student ID images for this pilot.
Describe precisely what was checked. A college email check proves email control, not advice quality or current residence.
Disclose recruitment, institutional, employer, or referral relationships on relevant profiles and offers.

Route one specific question to one willing helper first, with both parties' consent.
Offer another perspective only after the first exchange or an unanswered request; avoid broadcasting the same question to every resident.
Do not expose a helper's phone/email or create accounts for them without consent.
Use the new discovery, profile, connection, and messaging flow only after it passes release gates; before then, demonstrate locally and arrange consented manual introductions without pretending an app account exists.
Manage capacity in a local spreadsheet before adding scheduling software; use no paid scheduling service.
If no helper is available, show the real limitation and offer a waitlist with a clear expectation.

Capacity arithmetic: eight helpers at three new threads/week provide at most 24 threads/week; 25 provide at most 75.
Operate below 80% of confirmed capacity to allow absences and follow-up; each helper can lower their cap at any time.
This is asynchronous support capacity, not automatically paid-call availability.
If the product permits unrestricted requests, beta etiquette alone cannot enforce a safety control: use invitation pacing, monitor load, and ask engineering for a narrow limit only if actual abuse/load makes it necessary.

## 6. Fifteen discovery interviews

Complete 15 unique interviews: eight prospective movers, five residents/current students, and two community administrators.
Aim for five by 18 October, eleven by 25 October, and fifteen by 1 November.
Recontact five beta members 3–5 days after use; those follow-ups do not count as five additional unique interviews.
Use 15-minute sessions and consented written notes; recording is optional and requires explicit permission.

Ask about a specific recent question, the current workaround, how long an answer took, what made the person credible, and what remained unresolved.
Ask residents about the last unsolicited request they received, why they answered or ignored it, and the maximum help they want to provide.
Show the actual experience; observe whether a participant can find a relevant person without coaching.
Ask about willingness to pay only after the problem and current behavior are understood; hypothetical enthusiasm is not a purchase.
Record the negative cases: “WhatsApp is enough,” “I would not share this,” “I do not want repeated requests,” and “this is only useful once.”

For each interview, log: segment, recent job, workaround, trust concern, observed friction, proposed next test, and evidence confidence.
Do not infer willingness to pay, country coverage, or satisfaction from a participant accepting an interview.

## 7. First 100: one measurable funnel, three sources

Retain 100 real registered members as a stretch milestone: plan for 25 residents/helpers and 75 seekers only after a safe, reachable beta exists.
The 15 November review may instead show interviews, helper commitments, and a working local prototype; greenfield engineering can push live acquisition beyond that date.
Count one person once; the founder, test accounts, duplicates, and waitlist entries do not count.
The following arithmetic is a testable warm-channel hypothesis, not a benchmark:

| Source | Unique qualified landing visitors | Registrations | Assumed conversion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Personal/alumni resident invitations | 60 | 25 helpers | 41.7% |
| Administrator-approved seeker invitations | 120 | 45 seekers | 37.5% |
| Voluntary invitations forwarded after a useful exchange | 70 | 30 seekers | 42.9% |
| Total | 250 | 100 | 40.0% |

Qualified means a person fitting the selected corridor who visits after a relevant invitation; do not redefine qualification after seeing conversion.
Visitors must be deduplicated across channels; use first known source for this small pilot and label unknown attribution.
An administrator's approval, a post impression, or sending an invitation is not a visit or a signup.
The resident funnel assumes personal access; if that access does not exist, revise the target rather than mass messaging strangers.
If actual overall conversion is 20%, 250 visitors yield 50 signups and 500 visitors would be required for 100; do not buy traffic to conceal a weak offer.
Referral targets are especially uncertain because useful exchanges must happen first. If the referrals do not appear, report the miss and work on value before requesting more invitations.

## 9. Definitions that prevent vanity reporting

| Metric | Exact definition and reporting rule |
| --- | --- |
| Registered | Unique genuine successful account creation; waitlist and test accounts excluded |
| Onboarding activation | Completed profile plus a sent/accepted legitimate connection request or first contextual message within 168 hours of registration; count each user once; original working target 50; intent is not proof of value |
| Two-way conversation | Distinct participant pair with at least one delivered message from each participant; repeated messages do not create more pairs; launch target 25 |
| Substantive response quality | Review whether a real question received a relevant non-greeting reply; report this as a stricter subset, never silently replace the two-way-conversation definition |
| Useful outcome, the operating north star | Unique member explicitly reports their practical question helped, or founder records consented qualitative evidence, that week; report feedback coverage alongside helpfulness |
| First-response rate | Eligible new question threads receiving a substantive reply within 72 hours / all eligible question threads whose full 72-hour window elapsed |
| Time to first reply | Elapsed hours from the first legitimate request/message to the first counterpart reply; show median and 90th percentile among answered threads alongside unanswered counts and observation window |
| Days 2–7 return | Registered users doing a meaningful action 24–168 hours after signup / registrations observed for a full 168 hours; do not label this strict D7 retention |
| Strict D7 retention | Registered users doing a meaningful action 168–192 hours after signup / registrations observed for a full 192 hours; report separately only if sample supports it |
| Helper coverage | Members with at least one relevant, consenting helper currently open to requests / members seeking help in the cohort |
| Helper repeat participation | Helpers contributing in week one who contribute again in week two / helpers with both observation weeks elapsed |
| Operating burden | Founder minutes spent onboarding, matching, support, and moderation per useful outcome; report acquisition/content time separately |
| Safety workload | Reports received, first-review time, unresolved cases, and repeat abuse; never present zero reports as proof of safety |

Track unique users and pairs separately: 25 replied pairs do not necessarily mean 50 distinct activated users.
Keep positive/negative usefulness feedback separate from unanswered feedback; do not claim a satisfaction rate over everyone when only two people answered.
Keep body text out of analytics; use IDs, event times, outcomes, and optional one-click feedback. Any support access to private messages needs an explicit access policy.
Start with a local spreadsheet for consented research and recruitment. When the new app is ready, add only the minimal server-side events/queries needed for these definitions; no existing event system is assumed and no paid analytics platform is required.
Use registration-relative elapsed time: return windows are half-open intervals [24,168) and [168,192) hours; meaningful actions are relevant discovery, contextual requests/messages, substantive replies, or completed introductions.
These definitions follow IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md §8; use Asia/Kolkata for calendar summaries and UTC event timestamps.
At the 15 November review, seven-day activation and days 2–7 return are incomplete for registrations less than 168 hours old; strict D7 is incomplete below 192 hours.
For registrations through the end of 15 November, review seven-day activation after the end of 22 November and strict D7 after the end of 23 November, using exact registration timestamps.
Report counts, denominators, and windows. A small percentage with a small denominator is a learning signal, not statistically established product-market fit.

## 10. Pause, improve, or expand

Pause invitations immediately for a confirmed privacy/authorization leak, uncontrolled harassment/spam, broken core account access, or reports with no responsible reviewer.
Keep an urgent incident route and a named backup person; fix safety failures before promotion resumes.
When more than 20% of mature requests lack a reply after 72 hours, stop increasing seeker intake and inspect relevance, availability, and notifications.
Treat 72 hours as the internal pilot target until publicly supportable; do not promise it while the cohort is underperforming.
At fewer than 20 mature threads, inspect each miss manually instead of presenting an apparently precise trend.

Expand by one adjacent cohort only after two consecutive weekly reviews show:

- No unresolved launch blocker or unmanaged safety incident.
- At least 80% of mature question threads replied to within 72 hours, with at least 20 mature threads across those reviews.
- At least 60% helpfulness among at least ten feedback responses, with feedback coverage also disclosed.
- At least 20% days 2–7 meaningful return among at least 20 registrations observed for 168 hours, or a documented reason to test an episodic rather than recurring model; report strict D7 separately when supportable.
- At least half of ten eligible helpers repeat participation across two observed weeks.
- Enough confirmed spare helper capacity and a named support owner for the added cohort.

These are founder decision thresholds, not industry standards or evidence that a large market exists.
If replies are useful but seekers rarely return, test an episodic introduction service or community partnership before forcing a monthly subscription.
If users already solve the job comfortably elsewhere, narrow or stop; feature volume will not create a reason to switch.

## 11. Monetization after value: test one offer

Keep the first cohort's basic peer discovery free. Do not gate reporting, blocking, privacy, or deletion behind payment.
Follow IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md §9: the first revenue hypothesis is a fixed-scope organizer/community coordination service, fulfilled manually after the free cohort demonstrates useful outcomes.
This means earning from an organizer for a delivered service, not buying a hosting service or spending money on a pilot.
Interview the two discovery-stage administrators and three additional organizer prospects about a repeated coordination problem, current effort, budget owner, and willingness to pay.
Offer one interested budget owner a 30-day pilot covering a consented cohort setup, bounded onboarding coordination, helper-capacity tracking, and one weekly aggregate report.
State the member cap, helper responsibility, staffed support hours, exclusions, and a maximum five hours of setup/fulfillment; do not promise member outcomes or unlimited moderation.
At ₹0 budget, first seek a written, nonbinding expression of interest naming the offer, price, deliverables, and budget owner; it is not revenue or payment validation.
Seek one genuinely paid pilot and a renewal decision only after money collection, delivery obligations, and any required setup are separately authorized and feasible without an unapproved expense.
Do not configure billing or a payment processor now. No paid processor/provider is required to complete the current build or demand test, and no method is assumed universally free, legally adequate, or available.
Before collecting money, confirm actual capacity, total price, cancellation/refund obligations, support/data-access scope, and the appropriate lawful invoice/receipt and payment process. A commitment is not permission to create an unbudgeted liability.
An interest form or positive interview is not payment evidence; do not use a fake purchase success page.

Illustrative organizer offer: ₹5,000 potential gross revenue less five delivery hours and two sales hours valued at ₹500/hour leaves ₹1,500 after labor opportunity cost, before any real collection cost, tax, refunds, or other obligations.
Current approved cash spend remains ₹0; this calculation does not imply that ₹5,000 has been collected or can be collected free of charges.
If delivery takes eight hours instead of five, the same assumed offer leaves ₹0 after ten total labor hours, before those other costs.
Measure onboarding, support, moderation, and sales time. Narrow scope or revise an unaccepted price rather than hiding unpaid work.
These are pricing hypotheses, not proven margins. Recurring revenue requires an actual renewal and repeated contribution after all operating labor.

Optional later sensitivity case: a paid 30-minute lived-experience session, only if the organizer experiment has been reviewed and separate demand plus consenting helper capacity justify testing it.
Do not launch a call marketplace as the first revenue product. If tested later, no visa/admission/job guarantees, application ghostwriting, or unearned credential claims.

The following call economics are a cautionary later alternative, unavailable under the current ₹0-spend scope because they assume cash helper payouts and reserves; all values are assumptions:

| Item per completed session | Assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Customer price | ₹799 |
| Helper payout | −₹400 |

| Scheduling/support, ten minutes | −₹83.33 |

For this out-of-scope sensitivity only, ten hours of other operations valued at ₹500/hour creates ₹5,000 of monthly opportunity cost with ₹0 assumed additional hosting spend.

Do not mix free asynchronous helper capacity with paid-session capacity or use gross bookings as platform revenue.

Do not run organizer and paid-session experiments at once or build a paid dashboard without an interested budget owner and an agreed scope.
Exclude undisclosed university, lender, housing, or consultancy referral commissions from the beta model; commercial relationships must never silently determine who is recommended.
Do not sell personal data, private questions, or access to vulnerable newcomers.

## 12. What “low-touch income” would require

Approved additional cash budget: ₹0. First-100 learning example: 40 founder hours plus 25 helper hours, each valued hypothetically at ₹500/hour, equals ₹32,500 of labor opportunity cost and no assumed labor payment.
That is ₹325 per registration at 100 members, or ₹650 per onboarding-activated user at 50; these are different denominators, not cash acquisition costs.
Greenfield development hours are additional and must be tracked separately; this 65-hour learning example is not an estimate of total build effort.
Track actual time and any proposed expense before commitment. If a necessary tool cannot be used without new spending, defer it or change the operating scope.

| Stage | Automate or document | Human responsibility and stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 100 | Consent-based welcome templates, capacity sheet, event totals, repeat-question guide | Founder owns interviews, introductions, report review, incidents, refunds if any; no passive-income claim |
| After repeat outcomes | Reusable onboarding, helper topic/availability updates, opt-in reminders, weekly metric report | Named operator checks exceptions and safety queue daily; do not automate an unresolved workflow |
| After repeat positive contribution | Repeatable organizer onboarding/reporting; billing only after separate authorization and renewed paid pilots; tested runbooks and handover | Named support owner and backup remain; expansion requires spare capacity and economics compatible with the cash constraint |

Measure hours per useful outcome and organizer-pilot contribution over at least two completed paid pilots, with renewal evidence, before reducing founder involvement.
Target a falling support burden without a worsening reply rate, helpfulness, safety response, or refund rate.
Never claim that agent automation removes community moderation or that this plan guarantees passive earnings.

## 13. Human and agent work split

The founder is the accountable product, growth, and beta-support owner until a named person explicitly accepts a handover.
Publish actual staffed support hours and an acknowledgement expectation the founder can meet; do not advertise 24/7 human support.
Identify one backup before inviting people outside the personal network. If nobody can review reports, pause invitations.

Agents may summarize de-identified interview notes, calculate funnel counts, compare public primary sources, draft approved-channel invitation copy, and prepare the weekly decision memo.
Agents may propose themes from public discussions with links; a person checks the source and relevance before publication.
Agents must not scrape contacts, invent people/testimonials, impersonate helpers, send outreach, auto-post to groups, make consequential moderation decisions, or generate high-stakes personal advice as a resident.
An invitation draft is not permission to send. An analytics suggestion is not permission to export identifiable messages or contact lists to another service.
One owner assigns tasks with an input, output, acceptance check, and end condition; parallel agents research separate topics, while a reviewer challenges unsupported claims and mismatched arithmetic.

Daily founder routine: inspect incidents/reports, review unanswered mature questions, reconcile helper capacity, onboard only within that capacity, and log one concrete learning.
Weekly routine: review the actual funnel, five feedback excerpts without unnecessary identifiers, cohort retention, time spent, and one product/channel decision.
Keep a decision log with assumption, evidence, change, owner, and next review date.
